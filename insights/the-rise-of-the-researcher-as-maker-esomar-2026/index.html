The Rise of the Researcher-as-Maker: Crafting Bespoke Analytical Tools with AI

2026-07-17 · Training & Lectures

Last May, at the ESOMAR Tokyo 2026 event, DRK's Jay Ahn proudly presented an insightful session on "The Rise of the Researcher-as-Maker: Crafting Bespoke Analytical Tools with AI."

As the market research industry evolves rapidly alongside technological advancements, researchers are transitioning from merely being "users" of pre-packaged software to becoming "makers" of their own bespoke analytical tools. This transformation is heavily driven by AI capabilities that lower the barrier to entry for coding and software development.

Global Ethnographic Observation (GEO) Enhanced by AI

A key focus of our discussion was how this "Researcher-as-Maker" paradigm significantly empowers frameworks like Global Ethnographic Observation (GEO). When observing diverse cultures and consumer behaviors, especially in nuanced markets like Japan or Korea, generic tools often fail to capture the subtle, qualitative depths required for true insight.

By leveraging AI, researchers can craft custom analytical solutions—whether that involves specialized text analysis for local social media, unique video ethnography tagging systems, or real-time sentiment tracking tailored to specific cultural contexts. This means that GEO can be conducted with unprecedented agility and precision, ensuring that the unique nuances of local markets are faithfully translated into actionable global strategies.

The presentation highlighted practical case studies where custom-built tools bridged the gap between raw data collection in the field and strategic interpretation in the boardroom. The ability to iterate on these tools during an ongoing research project allows for a dynamic response to emerging trends—a crucial advantage in ethnographic studies.
