# The Rise of the Researcher-as-Maker: Crafting Bespoke Analytical Tools with AI

At the recent ESOMAR 2026 Tokyo conference, Direct Research Korea (DRK) presented a paradigm-shifting approach to modern consumer insights. The presentation, titled "The Rise of the Researcher-as-Maker: Crafting Bespoke Analytical Tools with AI", showcased how researchers are transitioning from passive users of standardized software to active \"makers\" of custom, agentic analytical tools. By leveraging advanced agentic platforms like Google Antigravity, analysts can now design lightweight, purpose-built tools that unlock deep, localized insights with unprecedented speed and accuracy.

1. The \"Maker\" Paradigm: From Software Consumers to Tool Creators

Traditionally, market researchers have been dependent on commercial SaaS platforms and standardized software providers for data analysis. However, in fast-moving industries like gaming, generic tools often fail to keep pace with dynamic communities or capture localized linguistic nuances.

The \"Researcher-as-Maker\" paradigm solves this constraint. Instead of waiting for tech providers to build features, researchers are now utilizing AI agents to act as their \"hands and eyes\" on the web. Through \"Vibe Coding\"—using natural language instructions rather than complex programming—analysts can quickly prototype, build, and adapt bespoke scrapers and analytical dashboards tailored to specific research goals.

2. Case Study: Longitudinal Gaming Sentiment Analysis on Inven

To demonstrate the impact of this approach, DRK shared a case study where they built \"Inven-Pulse\", a bespoke agentic tool designed to track sentiment for a major new game release on Inven (a leading South Korean gaming community).

Global sentiment analysis tools frequently miss the unique context of Korean gaming boards, where hardcore players discuss server stability, monetization ethics, and balance changes using heavy slang. The Inven-Pulse tool was built to automate the entire research lifecycle: Target Data Acquisition: The agent navigated deep web sub-pages, automatically expanding long-form \"Strategy & Tip\" threads and user reviews. Multi-Dimensional Feature Classification: Instead of generic positive/negative sentiment, comments were classified into specific game features (Combat Mechanics, Monetization/Gacha, Graphics, Server Stability). * Behavioral Correlation: The tool linked feature-specific sentiment to user retention markers (detecting churn signals like \"selling accounts\" vs. loyalty signals like \"buying the battle pass\").

The Result: The developer identified that player churn was driven specifically by \"inventory management frustrations\" rather than the core combat loop. Within a week, the developer issued a targeted hotfix, improving user retention by 15%.

3. Core Technology: Google Antigravity & The \"Glass Box\" Approach

Bespoke tools built under this framework rely on Google Antigravity's Autonomous Browser Actuation. Unlike traditional web scrapers that fail on dynamic JavaScript elements, Antigravity acts as a real browser—handling logins, expanding paginated comment sections, and navigating complex UI structures autonomously.

Furthermore, to maintain research integrity, the framework operates on a \"Glass Box\" model rather than a black-box algorithm. Antigravity generates comprehensive \"Verification Artifacts\"—including real-time reasoning logs and screenshots—allowing researchers to audit the agent's logic and ensure no slang or sarcasm was misinterpreted.

4. Broader Application Beyond Gaming

While the case study focused on the gaming sector, the Researcher-as-Maker framework is highly portable across various industries: K-Beauty & E-Commerce: Analyzing customer reviews on platforms like Olive Young or Amazon to map satisfaction with specific product ingredients or textures, and tracking repurchase intent. Travel & F&B: Monitoring dynamic Yelp or TripAdvisor reviews over time to identify specific reasons for service abandonment and operational paint points.

As we look to the future, the ability to design, adapt, and humanize the tools we use will define the next generation of insights professionals. The integration of bespoke, agentic tools marks the transition of researchers from reporting analysts to strategic technology partners.
